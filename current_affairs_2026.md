# Current Affairs & Volatile Facts — verified as of **September 2026**

> **Why this file exists.** The mock-test corpus was originally generated against a
> 2024-era view of the world, so a number of questions taught facts that have since
> changed — and several were scoped to the world when the syllabus scopes them to
> India or J&K. This file is the **single source of truth for every fact that can
> change over time**. The generator must consult it before writing any question in
> section B (GK & Current Affairs — India) or section C (GK — special reference to J&K).
>
> **AS-OF DATE: 2026-09.** Keep this date in sync with `AS_OF` in
> `mock-tests/_audit_scope.py` and the Factual Currency rule in
> `.kiro/steering/mock-test-prompt-constable.md`.

---

## How to use this file

1. **Prefer stable facts.** A question on the 1960 signing of the Indus Waters Treaty
   never rots. A question on "how many Ramsar sites does India have" rots within
   months. Where a stable question tests the same skill, write the stable one.
2. **If you must test a volatile fact, date it.** Put the as-of date inside the
   question stem — "As of August 2026, how many Ramsar sites…" — so the item is
   auditable rather than silently wrong.
3. **Never write an undated "currently / latest / most recent" claim.**
   `_audit_scope.py` flags these as `UNDATED`.
4. **When this file and your memory disagree, this file wins.** When this file is
   silent on a volatile fact, do not guess — pick a stable fact instead.

## 2. Indus Waters Treaty — status changed, this is the big one

The treaty itself (stable): signed **19 September 1960** at Karachi by
**Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Ayub Khan**, brokered by the **World Bank**. Eastern
rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India; western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to
Pakistan, with India allowed non-consumptive, limited-irrigation and
run-of-the-river hydro use. Permanent Indus Commission is the first-tier dispute
forum, then Neutral Expert, then Court of Arbitration.

**What changed:**

- **April 2025** — following the **Pahalgam terror attack** (26 killed, mostly
  tourists), India announced it was holding the Indus Waters Treaty **"in abeyance"**.
- **September 2026** — the **Court of Arbitration at The Hague** ruled that the
  treaty **remains in force** and that neither party may **unilaterally** suspend or
  terminate it, holding that the treaty text recognises no status of "abeyance".
- **India rejected the award**, disputing the court's jurisdiction and constitution,
  and the Ministry of External Affairs has reiterated that **the abeyance stands**.
  India also seeks modification of the treaty citing changed circumstances.

> **Corpus note:** any question framing the IWT as a smoothly functioning treaty that
> "has survived every war" or as a model of "successful water-sharing cooperation" is
> now out of date. The honest current framing is: *a treaty legally in force per the
> Court of Arbitration, but held in abeyance by India in practice and contested.*

## 3. USBRL, Chenab Bridge and Anji Khad Bridge — two different bridges

**These are two distinct structures. Never present one as an alias of the other.**

| | Chenab Bridge | Anji Khad Bridge |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Steel **arch** bridge | **Cable-stayed** bridge |
| Claim to fame | **World's highest railway arch bridge** — deck ~359 m above the riverbed, higher than the Eiffel Tower (330 m) | **India's first cable-stayed railway bridge** |
| River / location | Chenab river, **Reasi** district | Anji Khad (a Chenab tributary), Reasi district |

**USBRL (Udhampur–Srinagar–Baramulla Rail Link):**

- **272 km**, 36 tunnels and ~943 bridges; final cost ≈ ₹43,780 crore.
- **Inaugurated 6 June 2025** by the Prime Minister — both the Chenab Bridge and the
  Anji Khad Bridge were dedicated that day, completing rail connectivity between the
  Kashmir Valley and the rest of India.
- **Vande Bharat Express** services between **Shri Mata Vaishno Devi Katra and
  Srinagar** were flagged off the same day; regular services began **7 June 2025**.
  Katra–Srinagar journey time ≈ 3 hours.

## 4. India — UNESCO World Heritage Sites

- **India has 45 World Heritage Sites** as of **July 2026**: **37 cultural, 7 natural,
  1 mixed** (Khangchendzonga). India ranks **sixth** globally by number of sites.
- **45th site: the Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath, Uttar Pradesh** — inscribed
  **25 July 2026** at the **48th session of the World Heritage Committee in Busan,
  Republic of Korea**.
- **44th site: Maratha Military Landscapes of India** (2025) — a serial nomination of
  twelve forts across Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu.
- **43rd site: Moidams**, the mound-burial system of the Ahom dynasty, Assam (2024) —

## 7. RTI Act — the J&K angle (section C)

The syllabus lists the **RTI Act** under **section C (special reference to J&K)**, so
RTI questions in section C must carry a J&K dimension, not merely recite the central Act.

- J&K formerly had **its own** law — the **J&K Right to Information Act, 2009** (which
  replaced a weaker 2004 J&K Act) — because the central RTI Act, 2005 did not extend
  to the then-state of J&K under its special constitutional position.
- The **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** repealed the J&K RTI Act, 2009. Since
  **31 October 2019** the **central RTI Act, 2005 applies to J&K and Ladakh**.
- Consequently the **J&K State Information Commission was wound up**; appeals now lie
  with the **Central Information Commission (CIC)**.
- Central Act essentials (fair game once anchored to J&K): 30 days to furnish
  information, 48 hours where life or liberty is involved, ₹10 central application fee,
  Section 8 exemptions, Section 4 proactive disclosure, first appeal within 30 days,
  RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 empowering the Centre to fix Commissioners' tenure and terms.

## 8. Quick "do not test undated" list

These rot fastest. Either date them explicitly or avoid them:

- Officeholders of any kind (CM, LG, Governors, CJI, CEC, agency heads, captains)
- Counts of sites/reserves/schemes (World Heritage, Ramsar, tiger reserves, airports)
- "Newest member of", "latest state to", "most recent winner of"
- GDP shares, export figures, forex reserves, scheme outlays, population estimates
- Anything phrased "as of recent estimates" / "in recent years" with no year given
